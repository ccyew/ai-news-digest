AI is hitting entry-level jobs hardest, Stanford study finds

Hacker News AI · 2026-08-25 14:48 UTC

A Stanford study led by Erik Brynjolfsson finds that AI is disproportionately reducing entry-level employment, particularly in occupations reliant on "codified" knowledge—formal, documented skills learned through education—while roles built on "tacit" knowledge gained via experience and mentorship show faster growth for mid-career and senior workers. Using O*NET education requirements as a proxy, the researchers also found that occupations with more college graduates showed muted differences between high- and low-AI-exposure jobs, whereas in fields with few graduates, the most AI-exposed roles are declining outright. Brynjolfsson warns the effects are "real, persistent and widening," describing a labor market that maintains overall employment while "quietly closing the on-ramp" for new workers.
