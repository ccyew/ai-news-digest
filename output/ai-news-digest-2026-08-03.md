## 5. EU rules on AI models become enforceable. What's going to change?

**Source:** Hacker News AI | **Published:** 2026-08-02 19:40 UTC

The EU AI Act's provisions covering general-purpose AI models became enforceable, requiring transparency on how models were built, disclosure of copyrighted training data, and information for downstream developers, with additional risk-identification and mitigation duties for the most powerful "frontier" models. Enforcement falls to the newly created European AI Office, which faces limited resources and talent shortages and plans to lean on outside scientific experts and specialised AI safety firms; most major Western labs except Meta have signed the accompanying voluntary code of practice. Observers warn the rules could delay EU launches of advanced models by weeks and provoke friction with the Trump administration, while civil society groups urge the Commission not to focus enforcement solely on cyber and loss-of-control risks at the expense of fundamental rights concerns.

## 10. Generative AI floods and dilutes the market for books

**Source:** Hacker News AI | **Published:** 2026-08-02 11:06 UTC

A new study applied full-text AI detection to 14,419 self-published genre-fiction books sold on Amazon between 2023 and 2026, matched with daily sales data through June 2026, and found that titles with more than 25% detected AI text make up a large share of the catalog and a growing share of sales, increasingly occupying top-rank positions once held by non-AI books. Over the period, the number of books with recorded sales grew 19.2-fold while quarterly revenue rose only 8.9-fold, meaning revenue per selling book declined in most genres, with non-AI books losing the most ground where AI diffusion and Kindle Unlimited availability are highest. None of the books disclosed AI use, and the authors argue their findings speak directly to the market-effect prong of the fair use defense in AI copyright litigation.
