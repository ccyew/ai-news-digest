## 2. After Rippling blew millions on AI in months, it built an employee ROI tool

**Source:** TechCrunch AI | **Published:** 2026-08-07 21:30 UTC

Rippling launched AI Spend Console, a tool that tracks company AI token spending down to individual employees, teams, and roles, and correlates it with productivity signals like pull requests and code-review rework. The product grew out of an internal crisis: Rippling found it was on pace to burn 40% of its R&D headcount budget on AI tokens, with spend growing 80% month-over-month and one engineer costing $50,000 a month, while 10–15% of staff drove 60% of usage. After negotiating vendor caps and building an internal AI gateway to route prompts to cheaper models, Rippling cut token costs to about 15% of the R&D headcount budget while maintaining roughly 600 billion tokens of monthly usage. The tool is included for Rippling HR subscribers with usage-based fees, or sold standalone.

## 7. Cloudflare launches Kitesurf, a browser built for AI agents

**Source:** TechCrunch AI | **Published:** 2026-08-07 16:16 UTC

Cloudflare has released Kitesurf, a cloud-hosted browser built specifically for AI agents rather than human users, prioritizing context window management, token costs, and scalability over visual features like tabs and extensions. Built in 12 weeks on Cloudflare's Workers serverless platform using Blitz's rendering engine, Firefox's Stylo CSS parser, and the Boa JS Rust engine, the company claims it consumes significantly less CPU and memory than Chromium for tasks like screenshots and HTML extraction. Kitesurf is free during beta via Browser Run, and Cloudflare says it currently passes over 215,000 web platform tests and correctly renders sites including Wikipedia and Hacker News.
