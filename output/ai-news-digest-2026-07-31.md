## 9. AI productivity gains are closer to 10% than 10x

**Source:** Hacker News AI | **Published:** 2026-07-30 18:28 UTC

An analysis by DX of engineering velocity at 400+ companies (roughly 150–10,000 engineers) from November 2024 to February 2026 found that while AI tool usage rose an average of 65%, median pull request throughput increased just 7.76% — with a 13.1% mean and about 44% at the 90th percentile — putting typical gains in the 5–15% range rather than the 3x–10x promised by vendors. Developer interviews attributed the gap to coding being only ~16% of engineers' time, unassisted code review and integration absorbing time saved, a learning-curve "J-curve" for AI skills, and tools lacking institutional context. DX recommends assessing codebase and workflow readiness team by team, aiming AI at non-coding bottlenecks like planning, review, documentation, and operations, and tracking utilization, impact, and cost together.

## 21. OpenJDK Interim Policy on Generative AI

**Source:** Hacker News AI | **Published:** 2026-07-30 12:36 UTC

The OpenJDK Governing Board has approved an interim policy, drafted with corporate sponsor Oracle, banning any content generated in whole or in part by large language models, diffusion models, or similar deep-learning systems from OpenJDK contributions — including source code, text, and images in Git repositories, GitHub pull requests, emails, wiki pages, and JBS issues. Contributors may still use generative AI privately to comprehend, debug, review, and research OpenJDK code, and LLM-free spell-check or refactoring tools remain permitted; Oracle will propose a full policy later. Cited risks include reviewer burden from plausible-but-incorrect code, safety and security of the JDK, and IP conflicts with the Oracle Contributor Agreement, and Skara will soon add a mandatory compliance checkbox to every pull request.
