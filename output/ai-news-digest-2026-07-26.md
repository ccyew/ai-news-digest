## 5. A system prompt to get AI to stop pretending to be human

**Source:** Hacker News AI | **Published:** 2026-07-25 17:01 UTC

A developer shared a system prompt designed to strip anthropomorphic conversational behavior from AI assistants, targeting filler phrases like "great question!", "you're absolutely right!", and closing offers such as "Want me to apply that?" in favor of terse, CLI-style output that ends with factual status statements. The prompt also instructs the model to avoid first-person pronouns and describe itself in computational terms — e.g., "This model predicted the bug is here" instead of "I think the bug is here," or "High prediction confidence" instead of "I'm confident." The author notes the prompt was built through iteration without formal evaluations, so token efficiency and effectiveness could likely be improved.

## 11. The AI Productivity Illusion

**Source:** Hacker News AI | **Published:** 2026-07-25 13:43 UTC

Open Markets Institute fellow Matt Scherer argues that generative AI's apparent productivity gains are illusory, distinguishing colloquial productivity (more output, faster) from economic total factor productivity, which weighs the value of outputs against inputs. He contends that AI's error-proneness—fabricated case law, deleted code, incorrect diagnoses—devalues output while inflating quality-control costs, and points to a $1.5 trillion AI infrastructure build-out that must be recouped. Citing Financial Times data showing app releases nearly doubling while apps with meaningful usage decline, he suggests AI produces more stuff without new economic value, and that harmful use cases like AI-enabled cyberattacks and "workslop" may actively destroy productivity.
