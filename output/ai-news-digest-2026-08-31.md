## 1. Musk’s faster path to more gas turbines comes with pollution problem

**Source:** TechCrunch AI | **Published:** 2026-08-30 16:54 UTC

Elon Musk confirmed that SpaceX is building a "blades and vanes" foundry on roughly 830 newly acquired acres in Bastrop, Texas, to cast the single-crystal turbine components that are the key bottleneck in natural gas turbine production — a step he claims could accelerate turbine deployment by up to 18 months. Only four companies worldwide can produce these blades at industrial scale and all are booked, with GE Vernova sold out through 2030 as AI data center demand surges. The move would give Musk's ventures a manufacturing edge over rival hyperscalers, but faster gas turbine buildouts carry pollution risks: the NAACP has accused xAI of running unpermitted turbines in Memphis, and a Virginia study estimated one facility's eight turbines could cause 3.4 to 6.5 premature deaths annually and up to $99 million in health damages.

## 2. Texas Governor Abbott blocks funding for more Flock cameras

**Source:** The Verge AI | **Published:** 2026-08-30 15:35 UTC

Texas Governor Greg Abbott has frozen state funding for Flock's AI-powered license plate reader cameras, a move that came just before a Texas Tribune investigation revealed the state had spent over $30 million on the systems—money largely raised through a $1 fee added to insurance policies ostensibly to combat catalytic converter theft. Flock has faced mounting bipartisan criticism over privacy issues, data-sharing practices, unsecured video feeds, and at least six incidents in Texas of officers being suspended or charged for misusing the systems. Cities across the country, including several in Texas, have been canceling their Flock contracts amid the backlash.

## 3. Caterpillar is bringing to AI deployment what it learned from automating mining

**Source:** TechCrunch AI | **Published:** 2026-08-30 15:00 UTC

Caterpillar CTO Jaime Mineart said at the Ai4 conference that the company is extending lessons from decades of mining automation — autonomous haul trucks, dozers, and drilling — into more dynamic construction sites and quarries, while rolling out tools like the Cat AI Assistant that lets field technicians use voice commands to pull up repair procedures and identify parts. The systems draw on roughly 1.6 million connected assets and over 16 petabytes of structured data, and Caterpillar is also using AI agents to modernize legacy code and generate digital twins of manufacturing sites. The company plans to spend $100 million over five years training its 118,000 employees in AI, autonomy and robotics, as data center demand drove record quarterly revenue of $20.5 billion and a 72% jump in power-generation sales to $3.10 billion.

## 4. No AI Fridays

**Source:** Hacker News AI | **Published:** 2026-08-30 12:31 UTC

A campaign called "No AI Fridays" urges developers to disable AI coding assistants one day a week, citing research that heavy LLM use builds "cognitive debt," reduces engagement, and erodes critical thinking and skill development. Proponents argue the practice exposes blind spots created by offloading decisions to AI, surfaces opportunities for conventional automation, and cuts token spending. The htmx project's CEO has mandated the policy, and other companies can request to be listed by contacting the organizer on social media.

## 5. Fair Work Commission condemns 'plain wrong' AI legal advice

**Source:** Hacker News AI | **Published:** 2026-08-30 10:20 UTC

Australia's Fair Work Commission ordered sacked ALDI worker Sadnan Khan to pay $1,230 towards the supermarket's legal costs after finding he relied on "plain wrong" AI advice as a "quasi-legal advisor" and pursued a case with no real prospects despite repeated warnings — he even left AI chat instructions in his submissions. The tribunal said AI is not inherently problematic and can improve access to justice, citing Macquarie University lecturer Gregory Baker, who used a team of AI agents to become the first person to successfully challenge new casual employment laws. From 20 October, the commission will require applicants to disclose AI use and verify facts, authorities and hyperlinks, with consequences for non-compliance; Khan says he plans to appeal using multiple AI tools.
